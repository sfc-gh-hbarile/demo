Grant Portfolio & Opportunity Pipeline

State of grants and donor opportunities · Source: BP_DEMO.CURATED.DT_GRANT_PIPELINE · Submissions May 2024 – Nov 2026 · Prepared 18 Nov 2026 by Business Analysis & Data Science

Bottom line. The portfolio is healthy and well governed: $152.2M approved across 359 approved/paid grants, a 73.7% win rate on decided applications, and reviewer scores that strongly predict outcomes. The near-term issues are a 2026 decision backlog ($66.5M under review or submitted) and $12.5M of weighted donor pipeline attached to declined grants. These are the most actionable items in this report.
  • Donor pipeline: $88.7M open, $46.3M probability-weighted, $30.6M already committed.
  • Execution capacity is the dominant AI-flagged risk (41% of grants) and is linked to the lowest reviewer scores.
  • North America makes up 42% of grants and 48% of approved dollars, so the portfolio is geographically concentrated.

Insights & recommendations

Business analyst

1. Clear the 2026 review backlog

The 163 grants that are Under Review or Submitted represent $66.5M requested. 2026‑Q2 cycle time rose to 114 days, against 81 days on average. Fast-track applications scoring ≥ 3.0, since 97% of them are funded anyway.
Business analyst

2. Reconcile pipeline on declined grants

66 declined grants still carry open donor opportunities worth $12.5M weighted. The largest is Clean Grid Futures at $3.9M. Close these out or redirect them to an aligned grant, so that pipeline forecasts are not overstated.
Business analyst

3. Diversify geography

North America receives 48% of approved dollars. East Asia & Pacific has the highest average score (3.09) but the lowest approval yield ($9.0M of $27.5M). This points to an opportunity to grow in that region.
Data scientist

4. Invest in grantee execution capacity

Execution capacity is the primary AI-identified risk on 268 grants (41%), and these grants score lowest (avg 2.65). A capacity-building track could convert some of these marginal proposals into wins.
Data scientist

5. Formalize a score-based triage model

Score is a strong, stable predictor (r = 0.69). Grants scoring below 2 win only 6% of the time. A simple threshold model could route these low-scoring applications to referral early and save reviewer effort.
Data scientist

6. Don't use donor sentiment as a quality proxy

Sentiment is unrelated to reviewer quality (r = 0.01) and averages a modest 0.34. Track it as a separate relationship-health KPI, and look into why the 5-band grants have neutral sentiment.
